Every post on this site has a piece of artwork next to it. None of them are
images. Each one is an SVG drawn in code when the page is built, from two
inputs: the post's slug and its place in the archive.

## Two inputs

The first input is the post's **index**: its rank in chronological order, oldest
first. The index picks the composition, one of twelve (aurora, halo, dunes, orbs,
beams, bloom, ribbon, rays, mosaic, lens, spiral and stripes), and it picks the
two hues. Both advance together, so neighbouring posts differ in shape and in
color.

The second input is the **slug**, hashed with FNV-1a to seed a small random number
generator. That decides everything inside a composition: where the shapes sit,
how big they are, how they lean. So two posts that draw the same composition
still look unalike.

Indexing chronologically rather than by position on the page matters: a new post
appends to the end of that order, so publishing never reshuffles the artwork of
a post that's already out.

## Why not just hash the slug

It's the obvious move, and it doesn't work. A hash gives no spacing
guarantee, so the compositions clustered (one shape drew 4 of 13 posts) and the
hues piled up in one corner of the wheel, with 7 of 13 landing somewhere in
blue-purple. Golden-angle stepping, the usual fix for spreading hues, only
spreads consecutive integers; applied to hash values it does nothing.

Consecutive integers are exactly what the index is. Each step turns the first
hue a twelfth of the wheel, with a little jitter from the slug so the sequence
doesn't read as a color wheel, and the second hue sits at one of eight offsets
from the first, from nearly analogous to nearly complementary.

## Color

Each piece is a duotone. The two hues go on the `<svg>` as CSS variables, and
the stylesheet expands them into a fixed ramp: two background tints and six
inks, three on each hue.

```css title="app/global.css"
.art {
    --art-bg: oklch(0.88 0.138 var(--art-h));
    --art-bg2: oklch(0.84 0.152 var(--art-h2));
    --art-0: oklch(0.47 0.205 var(--art-h));
    --art-1: oklch(0.59 0.225 var(--art-h));
    --art-2: oklch(0.71 0.192 var(--art-h));
    --art-3: oklch(0.54 0.225 var(--art-h2));
    --art-4: oklch(0.67 0.2 var(--art-h2));
    --art-5: oklch(0.8 0.15 var(--art-h2));
}
```

It's `oklch`, not `hsl`, because lightness and chroma stay perceptually even as
the hue turns. The same numbers in `hsl` make the yellows glare and the blues go
muddy. Dark mode restates the whole ramp with its own numbers rather than
inverting it.

The ramp has to live on `.art`, not on `:root`. A custom property that contains
`var()` is resolved at the element that declares it, and only the result
inherits, so a ramp on `:root` resolves against an `--art-h` that doesn't exist
there. Every tile renders solid black.

Most of what keeps these from looking like charts is the overlap: soft
gradients, blur and translucent shapes laid over each other, so the two hues mix
into colors neither one contains.

## Try it

Type any seed, step through the index, and download what you like as a
wallpaper. A post's slug at its own index draws that post's artwork.
